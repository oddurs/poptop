# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be someone the rest of us want to keep working with.

## What that means here

**Argue about the work.** Strong disagreement about a design decision is the
point of this project — most of `docs/roadmaps/` is exactly that, written down.
Argue with the decision, the measurement, or the code. Not with the person who
wrote it.

**Bring evidence, and accept it.** A claim with a number behind it beats a claim
without one, whoever is making it. If someone measures your idea and it does not
hold up, that is the process working. The same applies in reverse: if you
measure someone's idea and it holds up, say so.

**Assume the other person is trying.** Contributors arrive with different
amounts of time, context, and English. A patch that misses the house style is
someone spending their evening on your project.

**Leave people their dignity.** No harassment, no demeaning or exclusionary
remarks, no unwelcome attention, no dragging someone's identity into a technical
argument. Public or private, in issues, pull requests, commit messages, or
anywhere else the project happens.

**Do not make the space worse for people who are not in the room.** Sustained
disruption, deliberate misreading in bad faith, and pile-ons all cost the
project people who never even opened the thread.

## If something goes wrong

Report it to the maintainers by opening a private report through GitHub, or by
emailing the address on the maintainer's GitHub profile. Reports are read by the
maintainers and are not shared further than needed to act on them.

You will get an acknowledgement. You may not get a detailed account of what
happened next — the outcome for the person reported is generally theirs, not
public — but you will be told that the report was handled.

## What happens

Maintainers will do what the situation calls for, and will say what they are
doing rather than acting silently. In rough order: a private word, a public
correction, a temporary removal from project spaces, or a permanent one.
Maintainers who behave this way are held to it more strictly, not less.

## Scope

This applies in project spaces — the repository, its issues, pull requests, and
discussions — and to conduct elsewhere that is directed at people because of
their participation here.
